Error trackers see stack traces. Opslane knows your product.

Opslane watches errors and session recordings from your app and turns them into a short list of issues. It picks the issues worth fixing and investigates them against your repository. A fix that builds and passes your tests in a sandbox opens as a pull request. When it can't fix something, it says why. A daily digest keeps you out of the dashboard.

## Who it is for

Teams who own a production web app and review their own pull requests. Opslane runs on your infrastructure, so you decide what it may touch and where the data goes.

## When to use it

- A bug reached users and you want the fix waiting for you rather than a ticket.
- Your error tracker holds thousands of events and five of them matter.
- Users abandon a form or click a dead button, and the console stays silent.
- You want a record of what ran and what passed before a change reaches review.

## How the docs are organized

**Get started** covers setup: self-hosting the stack and installing the SDK. **How Opslane works** explains the system for someone deciding whether to trust it. **Issues** and **Fixes** cover the two halves of the product loop. **Projects and keys**, **Notifications**, and **Privacy and trust** each cover one area of configuration.

## What Opslane is not

- **Not an APM or metrics backend.** Opslane ingests application errors and the session context around them: breadcrumbs, network timing, and optional replays. It does not do latency percentiles, distributed tracing, or infrastructure monitoring.
- **Not autopilot.** Opslane opens pull requests but never merges them. Review and merge stay with the repository owner.
- **Not a dashboard to babysit.** Conclusions ship as pull requests and issues; the dashboard exists for replays and settings, not triage duty.
- **Pre-1.0.** The [`POST /api/v1/events` wire contract](/contracts/events/) is append-only and backward-compatible. Other interfaces may still change before 1.0.
